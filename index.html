    About the club

    A student-run engineering club at Clovis Community College that competes, builds and teaches.

    Engineering Renaissance is a student-run engineering club at Clovis Community College, dedicated to promoting excellence in STEM and higher education. Our mission is to further STEM in the Central Valley and showcase Clovis Community College internationally. Together, we keep showing that Crush has depth.

    The club brings mechanical, electrical, software and business students together as one team. Through competitions like MATE ROV, hands-on projects and community outreach, members gain real experience with engineering concepts, CAD software, programming and technical skills that prepare them for careers and for transferring to four-year universities.

    Projects

    Engineering is learned by building. Our projects take members from their first breadboard to competition-ready systems, with something real to show at every stage.

    Intro projects

    The starting point for every new member. These first group projects need no prior experience: you work with teammates on real hardware and software from day one and build the skills every later project uses.

    Members learn embedded systems on Arduino and ESP32 microcontrollers: reading sensors, driving motors and writing firmware for the physical world. On the design side, 3D modeling in Onshape and SolidWorks teaches you to think spatially, design for manufacturing and iterate quickly.

    • Arduino
    • ESP32
    • Onshape
    • SolidWorks
    • Embedded systems
    • 3D modeling

    Halloween projects

    The next step after intro projects: a more technical prompt with a seasonal twist. These projects raise the complexity and give members more hands-on time.

    Think animatronics, sensor-triggered scare machines, LED displays and sound-reactive installations, all built from scratch with the skills from intro projects. This is where members start making engineering decisions on their own.

    • Animatronics
    • Sensor integration
    • LED systems
    • Creative engineering

      Outreach and collaborations

      Engineering should reach beyond the lab. Through collaborations with other clubs and community partnerships, we bring hands-on STEM to the Central Valley.

      EEG-controlled prosthetic hand

      With the MEDLIFE Club and MESA

      Biology and engineering students are designing and building a robotic prosthetic hand controlled by EEG brain signals, for amputees who need one.

      Engineering students handle the embedded systems, signal processing and mechanical design. Biology and pre-med students from MEDLIFE bring neuroscience and human-factors expertise. MESA gives students from underrepresented groups a way into the research.
